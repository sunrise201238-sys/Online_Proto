# Gun VS Gun

A fast-paced 1v1 / 2v2 arena prototype with two main modes — **Duel** (single stock) and **Trio** (three-unit rosters). Auto-aim — no manual targeting. The fight is about resource management: when to sprint, when to dodge, when to break line of sight, when to fire.

## Modes

Two main modes, each playable **1v1 or 2v2**, offline and online:

- **Duel** — classic single stock: one unit per fighter; a team loses when all its fighters are down.
- **Trio** — three-unit stock: every slot (human or bot) fields an **ordered roster of three units**, repeats allowed. When a unit dies, the slot's next unit respawns at its original spawn point with the standard 3 s spawn immunity; the killer keeps position / HP / boost — no kill reward. A team loses when every roster on its side is spent. Each fighter's remaining units show as a row of small weapon renders under their side's HP bars (one line per team member; Duel shows its single unit the same way), and a small golden glow-bar marks each Trio line's currently fielded weapon.
- **Spectating (2v2, both modes)**: if you're out for good while your ally fights on, the camera follows the ally with your own-unit visual kit (rear art + through-wall X-ray); the lock reticle stays up and mirrors the ally's actual target (the TARGET button goes inert), and the boost bar and ammo readout follow the ally too (2026-09-29 — they used to stay frozen on your dead unit's numbers).

Orthogonal to all of the above, every player also picks a **view mode** — **Shooter** (the chase camera you pilot directly; named "Classic" until 2026-10-10) or **Command** (a commander's diorama view where your unit fights on its own bot brain and you give it orders). See the **Command mode** section below.

### Offline
- **1v1**: you vs one enemy bot.
- **2v2**: you + an ally bot vs two enemy bots. Friendly fire is off between teammates.
- **Trio picks**: you select your three units in order, then each bot's three — same selection grid, titles count up (1/3 → 3/3).
- Optional "Dummy" mode on the map-select screen — zeroes out damage from every bot (enemies and your ally), so you can practice movement and observe bot behaviour without dying.
- Optional "Spectator" mode beside it — a bot takes over your unit and you watch the match: **TARGET** cycles the camera across every unit on the field (both teams), the HUD (HP / boost / ammo) follows whoever you're watching, the edge arrows stay viewer-relative, and the end banner reads **TEAM 1 WINS / TEAM 2 WINS**. Stacks with Dummy: the bot driving your slot still deals damage exactly as you would, and every other bot's shots are blanks (2026-10-03 — before that the combination zeroed everyone for a no-deaths exhibition).
- The main menu also carries a **Guide** button (a popup summarising controls, mechanics and the command-mode gestures) and a **Server Boot-up** button that opens the online debug panel.

### Online
- **Mode selection**: the host picks **Duel or Trio**, then **1v1 or 2v2**; joiners inherit the lobby's modes.
- **1v1**: the host presses **Start Match** when ready. The opponent slot holds a bot (default **Saori / Unit 1**) until a second human queues in and takes it — start early to play the bot, or wait for a player.
- **2v2**: the host presses **Start Match** when ready; empty player slots fill with bots (2v2 rooms open as Saori / Hoshino / Aru / Atsuko by slot; Trio bots field three copies of that unit unless the host picks otherwise). Up to four humans can play (any split between teams); bots fill any remaining slots.
- **Bot unit selection**: in the lobby, the host can tap any bot slot to pick which unit that bot plays — in Trio, its three units in order (default: three copies of the slot's usual unit). A human joining the slot always overrides the bot.
- **Trio queue room**: every slot's roster is shown in three lines and updates live as picks land.
- **View mode**: each player picks Shooter or Command in the queue room; the pick is final once the match starts and is never shown to the other team (teammates see it as a gold **[CMD]** tag on the roster line). Details in the Command mode section.
- **Slot-colored corner HUD** (online, 2026-08-22): the corner HP bars follow the **server slot** — left column team A (p1 top / p3 bottom), right column team B (p2 top / p4 bottom), each bar tinted its slot color (p1 blue / p2 pink / p3 green / p4 orange), the same picture for every viewer and spectator (1v1: p1 left, p2 right); your own bar wears a thin **white rim**. The command diorama's markers, cards, rings and lock triangles use the same slot colors online (offline keeps the role palette).
- **Host migration**: if the host leaves in the lobby or at the end menu, the longest-waiting player is promoted to host (their unit picks carry over; the lobby keeps its Duel / Trio and 1v1 / 2v2 settings and the new host re-chooses the map).
- Multi-lobby — when an existing lobby is full or running, new joiners spawn their own lobby and become host.
- Team swap in 2v2: any non-host player can `Join` an empty slot to switch teams (e.g. two humans want to co-op on one side against two bots).

### Unit profile card

The first tap on a unit card opens its profile beside the grid (tap the same card again to confirm, anywhere else to cancel): the character art on the left and the **weapon panel** on the right — the gun's real-world name, its render, and since 2026-09-22 a **3×2 stat grid** under the render (owner pick "C" from three in-game samples: a third plate beside the panel and a label/value row list were the other two; the demo line carries the same stats as a row list). The six cells read live from the unit data:

- **Mag**, **RPM** and **Reload** (seconds) as plain numbers.
- **Dmg** shows every tier where a gun has them: Aru's PSG1 `50/35/20` (far / mid / near), Kei's Railgun `30/20` (quick / charged), the shotguns per pellet `3 ×8`.
- **Movement** is the walk-speed tier word (walk speeds retuned 2026-09-24): **Fast** (walk 16 — Atsuko, Marina, Yuuka, Hoshino, Haruka, Rabu), **Medium** (12 — Saori, Asuna, Subaru, Fubuki, Aris, Aru, Kei), **Slow** (Koyuki and Kurumi at 10, Hina at 8).
- The **Spread picture** is a seeded mini-simulation of a **walking spray at a common 60-unit reference** (the shotguns at their own 40): the ring is a standing target's half-width at that distance, the orange dots are up to 30 rounds — magazine-limited — each leaving on the cone the sim gives that round (base + bloom, per-round bloom up to the cap, and the between-round recovery of the no-delay marksman rifles), fading with round order so the first rounds read brightest. The spray stops at the first round whose cone no longer fits the frame (2026-09-22, owner pick from samples — pinning spilled rounds to the frame's edge had piled Fubuki's 27 out-of-frame rounds into a rectangle), so nothing is drawn outside the frame: Saori's whole 30-round spray stays inside the ring, Koyuki's and Hina's pictures end a few rounds short of the magazine with their late rounds at the frame's edge, and Fubuki's and Aris's show their first three quick rounds — one on the ring's centre, two at its edge — because the fourth already flies wider than the frame; the shotguns show their 8-pellet pattern (the Beretta 1301 with its 1.4× horizontal stretch — Hoshino's since the two shotguns swapped patterns on 2026-10-10).
- A unit may carry a **note** (`cardNote`, user 2026-10-24): one line at the card's bottom-left, under the art. Kurumi's reads "This unit has 20% more health." A map may carry one too (`cardNote` in the client's `MAP_DATA`; District's reads "Unit has more stamina in this map.", user 2026-10-29), shown on its info card the same way, under the picture.

The grid keeps the same sizes on phones (checked at 390 px: nothing wraps or spills).

### Random & All Random cards

Both offline and online pickers carry them:

- Every unit grid has a gray **Random** card (question-mark thumbnail): it rolls a unit the roster being built doesn't already contain (different players can still land on the same unit). The map picker is two sections, each with a Random card of its own: **Scene Maps** — the maps cut from modelled scenes (Town, Spacecraft) — over **Concept Maps**, the hand-made arenas; a section's Random rolls among that section alone and skips **Shooting Range** and **Plain Field** (`noRandom` in the map table; user 2026-10-19). A map's row may say which part of its card picture the wide thumb shows (`thumb: 'top' | 'bottom'`; the thumb box cuts a square picture to a band, the centre by default) — Backrooms and Warehouse show their upper part, Spacecraft its lower; the profile shows the whole picture (user 2026-10-24).
- Every unit grid also carries a golden **All Random** card: one confirm fills every remaining unit slot of the current flow at once and advances it (offline: the rest of your roster, then every bot picker that follows — Duel included; online: the rest of your roster). The map picker deliberately has no All Random.

## Sudden Death

Picked as the **rules mode** beside the view mode (**Health Point** is the normal game; the two were named Normal / Classic until 2026-10-10). Everyone fights at **1 HP** — one landed round decides — and Trio respawns arrive at 1 HP too. The two snipers (Aru, Kei) are out of the pickers and the Random cards. The match opens on a full-width **SUDDEN DEATH** banner with the simulation frozen behind it; the 3 s spawn immunity and every bot slot's 650 ms first-shot delay count from the banner's end (the server holds the same way online).

**The Sudden Death bot brain** — one module for both hosts, `shared/src/sim/sdbrain.js`: the server's `tickBot` and the offline client's `updateEnemy` each build a small host and call `sdBrainTick` once a tick, for every bot slot offline and online, 1v1, 2v2, Trio and the spectated player slot; there is no second copy and no switch. Rewritten on 2026-10-16 (b1017a–h) as **one rule in four parts, each written once**, after the earlier brain had grown to 3,000 lines and 144 knobs of special cases: the unit stands only where it is hidden; it is exposed only to buy something — a firing line, a better cell, the way back; every exposure is priced the same way, and the cheapest one that buys the most is taken. 1,000 lines, 44 knobs (`BOT_SD`), nothing per weapon or per map; the enemy's magazine is never read, its shots are.

1. **Hidden** — one test of the body's silhouette (the front of the capsule, both shoulders, the four shoulder corners, the top) against every **eye**: each enemy where it stands, where its run takes it in the next 0.5 s, and where it fired **every round still in the air** (a shot log per enemy, each shot kept for a round's life — a sprinting sprayer has three or four rounds in the air at once, each from a different place). Hidden means no eye reaches any point of the silhouette.
2. **The price** — the chance of dying in an exposure of a given length at a given distance, moving across the line, under the enemy's reaction: none while its stream is on, 250 ms while its gun is aimed (it fired within 2 s), 600 ms when it is not, or the median of its last five measured reactions, whichever is faster; with its live bloom and cadence, the shotguns by their real cloud. Against it, the chance the unit's own rounds land in the same window. An attack is taken when the deaths it costs to land one hit stay under the one cap, **0.15**; a route, when its death chance does.
3. **The plan** — hidden with nothing under way, the unit prices **the Engage Run** (a straight run across the line through a firing point to a hidden end), **the Stand Peek** (a leg to a firing point, the window, the way back: inside **30 u** the leg is the dodge itself, **the Close Peek**, beyond it a sprint with the dodge kept for the way out) and a **relocation** (a hidden cell by a priced route: closer while beyond the unit's reach, a line-capable cell inside it, else any cell nearer), and takes the cheapest under the cap. Every option refused, it asks what it is waiting for, priced once more with what time brings back — the gun gone quiet, the dodge in hand, the tank full: something under the cap then makes the plan **the Wait** (pacing its cell inside a 6 u leash, priced again every 250–500 ms); nothing makes it **the Approach**, the pathfinder's route to the enemy, priced like any route. The cell it waits in is read at the horizon as well: seen by then, **the Escape** — the nearest cell hidden through the horizon, by a priced route, before the line opens. Caught exposed with no plan, **the Reaction**: the dodge across the line on the tick the line opens, then the sprint to the nearest hidden cell.
4. **The legs** — hidden, along the route; exposed, the crossing legs, always across the line, the side flipped only by a wall (physics' note of the face it pushed the body out of). A peek's window runs across the line away from its cover and ends the tick its way out would cost the cap. The brain and the gun keep running through the dodge; only the body's velocity stands down for the step.

**The unit's own reaction** (b1017f–g): the trigger opens **250 ms** after the unit *knew* — an attack's take (the Stand Peek and the Engage Run walk it into sight: a leg longer than the reaction fires the tick its line opens, a shorter one waits out the rest), else the first sighting of its silhouette. The hosts take this trigger as the Sudden Death bot's only trigger — the plain brain's bloom gate does not apply — and the own-hit price counts rounds from the same moment, so a window too short for its first round is refused rather than walked.

**The tank** (b1017d, b1017h): a reserve of **86** (a dodge, 48, and half a second of lateral sprint) is kept through every planned exposure, which starts only with its own cost on top of it; the body exposed spends to the floor, 8. Hidden legs sprint through **the valve** — the tank is spent in one draught and refilled in one: the valve shuts when the gauge is down to the reserve plus the longest peek's sprint and opens again only at a full gauge. (A hidden leg used to sprint a tick every few hundred ms as the gauge refilled, each tick resetting the 500 ms refill pause, so the gauge never rose while the unit walked and attacks went out on a third of a tank.) Defensive spending — the dodge, the crossing legs, the retreat, the escape — does not pass the valve.

**Reach** — the one per-unit number the brain still reads: how far the unit's own gun lands every round on a standing target (the base cone's footprint fits the hit radius; a shotgun's lock range stands in; `sdReach` in the unit data overrides the formula). Attacks are searched inside the reach plus 10 u of slack; beyond it the relocation closes in. The engage floor and the band of the earlier brain are gone: a route's or an attack's safety is its price, not a distance.

**Measured** (headless, the shared sim driven tick by tick): against the brain it replaced, 63% of decided duels at b1017e and about 50% once the unit's own reaction was in (the old brain reacts in 0 ms); the wall-hugger scan on flashpoint (the human 1.2 u off every obstacle face) 29–30 of 31 kills in 40 s; 10 ms of CPU per bot per second (was 14). The equivalence test (`shared/test/sdbrain_equiv.test.js`) plays the live brain against a frozen copy tick for tick on seeded matches, so a port between lines changes nothing. **F9** in the client prints the Sudden Death report to the console — the plans taken, the pricings refused, the rounds fired, and the last five seconds as a tape — and a bot's death prints the same with the killing round's geometry.

**Open observations** (2026-10-17, from playtest death reports; none changed yet — a single report never justifies a change, and every change must be a rule, not a patch):

- **The Reaction priced, not ordered.** Caught exposed, the unit always dodges and then runs to the nearest hidden cell; it never chooses to stand and fire. Three deaths in narrow corridors came at the turn-around right after the dodge, the body passing back through the point the gun was already on. The run and the stand should be priced by the same function (the route's exposure against the trade, deaths per hit) and the cheaper taken; a fixed order would fight where three steps reach cover.
- **The dodge's side and the run's side.** The dodge takes the side with the longer open run; the escape search then finds the nearest hidden cell, usually back where the unit came from, so the body reverses. Candidate: among the sides with room, the one toward the cell it last stood hidden in.
- **A gun is aimed when it has seen the unit, not only when it has fired.** A hunter who sprints without firing reads as a cold gun (600 ms), so escape routes with exposed stretches price low; he fires the moment the unit lands. The brain already knows when it was last seen.
- **A route under way keeps its first price.** Only the Approach is re-priced at the cadence; a relocation's exposed stretches are priced once, and a sprinting enemy changes them within a second. Candidate: every route re-priced from where the body is, at the cadence, like the Approach.
- **The Approach's route and the price of waiting.** When every option is refused, the Approach prices the shortest path, which crosses the open; the safest path (the exposure-weighted search the relocations already use) would pass more often. If stalemates remain, waiting needs a price of its own — the longer the duel stays undecided, the dearer the wait — compared against the safest route by the same function. Needs a stuck report (F9) first.
- **The crossing legs lean 45° toward their goal** (`reactDriftDeg`), against the rule that every sprint runs across the line. Held on the owner's word.
- **The jump walks the whole route ahead of it** regardless of the tank; a route with a jump link is walked while the gauge is full.
- **The dead-end pocket** on flashpoint, N(112, 81.35): the grid has no cell there and the Approach's straight last leg clips a corner. A map change, held.
- Two guards that cannot fire (`taken ?? now`, `Array.isArray` on the shot log) to be deleted with the next change; one report with a geometry the tape cannot explain (a perpendicular dodge whose ends both sit 1.1 u from the killing round's line).

**The one-brain experiment** (2026-10-17): the Sudden Death brain priced in life — a hit costs damage / HP of a life, so one brain and one price serve both modes — replaced the plain brain, was measured (against the plain brain: 69% wins, a 30.8 s median kill against 16.3 s, five times the CPU) and was shelved the same day: the plain brain is cheap, predictable and the easier game. It is archived on branch `Testing_0.9.0_New-BOT-Brain` (`docs/NEW_BOT_BRAIN.md` there: the principle, the structure, the reports, the pending designs). The plain brain here is that work's first two steps: its dead code deleted and one module for both hosts (`shared/src/sim/nbrain.js`), behaviour unchanged.


### Free pov (Shooter mode, b1027c, user 2026-10-30)

Shooter mode has two points of view, switched any time without the pause menu: `i` on the desktop, two quick taps on the empty screen on a phone (ignored while Command mode stands). **Angled pov** is the classic one: the chase camera frames the unit and its lock, every shot is aimed at the lock. **Free pov** keeps the angled pov's own camera rig (13 behind, 6.8 up, a shoulder to the right, looking at the mid-point to the lock, the field of view by the lock's distance — with the crosshair level the two cameras stand and look alike at every range, b1027i), turned to the aim and swung by its pitch about the unit itself (b1027j: swung about the far look point, a glance down lifted the camera into a bird's view), snapped with no lag, and a **crosshair** at the screen centre says where the rounds go: the shot is the line from the muzzle to the point under the crosshair (the first wall, or the enemy body the camera ray crosses before it), so it holds even up close. The player aims the crosshair. Desktop: the mouse (the pointer lock when the page's frame allows it, otherwise a drag with a button held), left click fires; `Shift` sprints (a double-tap locks it), `Q` dodges, `E` switches the lock, beside the older K / L / U (b1027f). Phone: a swipe on the empty screen, or a drag on the fire button, the sniper's charge included. Sensitivity (`window.__freePov.cfg`, live): 0.25°/px for a swipe or a fire-button drag (a landscape phone's width ≈ 200°), 0.10°/px for the mouse, pitch −50°…+60°. The lock is kept but not fired at: the unit turns to the crosshair while it fires or charges (the crosshair's own direction, not the shot's line, which leaves the muzzle a few degrees off it when the aimed point is near — b1027k) and otherwise faces the way it moves, holding its heading when still (b1027f), there is no red lock and no homing, the lock bracket hides, and the crosshair ring is the rounds' cone drawn on the screen (the base spread plus the bloom, as the rounds sample it; a shotgun's ring is fixed at the small base size every gun opens with — b1027h, user 2026-10-30: its rounds are one rigid pattern, not a cone; never under 12 px), red on an enemy's body (b1027e, user 2026-10-30: the camera rig, the facing, the ring and the keys). A round hits whichever enemy it meets (each tick its target is the living enemy nearest its flight: the sim's `retargetFreeAim`, the client's mirror); it still carries an *intent target*, the enemy nearest the aim line, for the bookkeeping, and a sniper charge is marked with it. Its glint warns **every** enemy: bots react as if aimed at, online every human enemy sees it. Back in angled pov the lock goes to the enemy nearest the crosshair — the smallest angle off the camera's centre ray; two within half a degree, the nearer one (b1027l, user 2026-10-30; before, the nearest enemy). Online the frame carries `freeAim`, `aimYaw`, `aimPitch` and `lockTarget` (the picked slot; the server takes it only for a living enemy), and the server fires along the aim it is sent. The TARGET button hides while the player's own free pov stands — no lock to switch — and a spectator keeps it to change the watched unit; a spectator's own `i` does nothing — the spectator-mode watcher, a dead player watching a teammate and an online spectator socket alike (b1027o) — and the lock bracket follows the watched unit's pov — shown while it is in angled pov, gone while it free-aims (b1027n, user 2026-10-30); watching a unit in free pov, the spectator sees that unit's crosshair drawn where its aim line lands on the spectator's own screen (the snapshot carries a free-aiming unit's `aimYaw`/`aimPitch`, three decimals, only while it free-aims; b1027m, user 2026-10-30). Universal: every map, every unit, offline and online alike.

## Command mode

The alternative to the classic chase camera (2026-08-21): a tilt-shift **diorama overview** of the whole map where the units fight on the standard bot brain and you play the commander — observe, select, order. The picker is a Shooter / Command chip in the menus; like Duel / 1v1 the choice is session-only — every site open starts Shooter. Offline, `V` or the pause menu also toggles it mid-match, and solo 2v2 play commands **both** of team one's units; online the pick happens in the queue room, is final once the match starts, and commands your own unit **plus a bot-filled teammate slot** (2026-08-22 — human teammates are never commandable; details in Command mode online below).

**Orders** — three kinds, layered on top of the bot rather than replacing it:

- **Move order** — tap your unit (selection glow), then tap anywhere on the map; or drag from the unit and release. Where floors stack (a bridge deck over ground), **hold** the tap to cycle the vertical layer — the stack lists only standable floors, and the preview auto-picks the lowest reachable one. The unit pathfinds to the spot, **fighting along the way but never abandoning the destination** — travel is **pathfinder-guided the whole way** (2026-08-22): the route re-plans from the unit's current position after every combat reflex and on a 1.5 s cadence, and the unit **vaults the route's jump-links** (raised platforms, fence gaps) instead of grinding at the ledge. On arrival it holds the area in an Engage-style orbit anchored on a **radius-12 ring** for **40 s**, then returns to full autonomy; the orbit **turns around at walls** (a ring straddling a fence — Airport's rim glass, say — patrols the reachable arc instead of grinding the pane), and a unit knocked out of the area by a Defense escape **returns as a fresh pathfinder-guided travel leg**, resuming whatever remains of the 40 s window. An unreachable spot draws a red **"Area is not available"** note at the tap and keeps your selection for an immediate retry. The standing ring is also a live handle (2026-08-27, nothing selected): **grab it anywhere on its disc and drag** to move the order (the whole circle plus a finger-sized margin counts at any zoom — a tap inside the area still orders a *selected* unit there, since ring grabs only engage with nothing selected) — the ring brightens in its unit's color the moment you pick it up (overlapping rings resolve to the nearer center; a dead tie hands it to your own unit), the same preview / validation / layer rules apply, and releasing re-issues the order with a **fresh 40 s window** (any drag counts, even released back where it started — there is no in-place cancel; an unreachable release just draws the red note and leaves the old order untouched) — or **double-tap the ring** to withdraw the move order alone (a standing force lock survives).
- **Force lock** — with your unit selected, tap an enemy: the unit locks that target until **either party dies**, then falls back to its own target-finding. The pinned enemy wears corner **triangles in the commanding unit's color** (the X layout; when two allied commanders pin the same enemy, the later lock wears the **+** edge-midpoint layout instead of splitting corners). Tapping the same enemy again cancels the lock; with **nothing selected**, tapping the pinned enemy's marker or card releases **every lock your units hold on it** at once (2026-08-27 — covers the bot teammate without selecting it; a human teammate's lock is never yours to drop).
- **Hide** (2026-09-26) — **select** your unit (tap its marker or its card — the selection glow) and a shield appears centred just above its **info card** (below it at the top edge) for as long as the selection stands; **tap the shield** to hide. It reads lit while the unit already hides. No hold anywhere. **Hide** wipes the unit's move and lock orders; **any new move or lock order releases it** (the order then stands as usual), as does a double-tap on the unit. A manual reload of 3 s or more enters Hide on its own for the reload (see *Reload hide* under the bot brain) — unless a move order stands, in which case it runs a Cover Hide and keeps the order. A hiding unit's first priority is line of sight: every tick it checks whether **both live enemies** can see it; if they can, it walks the shortest route (a walk-only Dijkstra over the nav grid, no distance cap, capped at 600 cells per search, at most one search per 0.5 s per unit and one per tick per match) to the nearest cell hidden from both. Once hidden it never stands still (owner: a statue looks awful): it **paces** its cover at walk speed on short legs that are verified hidden *before* they are taken (a 2.5 u look-ahead eye test against every enemy eye plus the walk rule), on a 6 u leash around the spot, biased away from the nearest enemy, re-picking its heading every 0.35 s. It also **watches the enemies move**: each is projected 0.5 s ahead on its live velocity, and an eye that would see the unit from there — or a nearest enemy inside 26 u closing at more than 2 u/s — triggers a **slip** to a cell hidden from the live *and* predicted eyes (when closing, one at least 6 u farther from that enemy), before the unit is ever seen. Searches run one attempt per tick (the fallback tiers follow on the next ticks) on the same 0.5 s cadence, so the per-tick cost stays at a handful of sight tests. It **does fire back** whenever a shot exists from where it stands, and never moves for a shot. Being hit does **not** trigger Defense while hiding (hit-stun still applies; the anti-glint dodge and the sniper charge rules are untouched) — **unless no spot hidden from both enemies exists within the search budget** (2026-09-27: the nearest-enemy-only fallback is gone — a walking target hidden from one enemy measured worse than Defense): then it fights on its pursue legs, a fresh hit runs the plain Defense escape, and it keeps searching every 0.5 s until such a spot appears. Sprints on the way are latched: a dash arms only above **50 boost** and may spend down to the **8-boost** hard floor. Released, the unit is plain autonomy again — no badge. Online the order travels as `order:stance` (same per-unit 0.5 s rate limit), lives in the team-scoped command side-table like the other two, and never reaches the enemy; a landed `order:move` / `order:lock` releases it server-side.
- One command per selection — the glow drops as soon as an order lands. **Double-tap** your unit to clear both orders at once; a slow re-tap just deselects.
- **Cover Hide** (2026-09-26, reload-only since 2026-09-27) — a unit under a move order spends a **long manual reload (3 s+)** in a hide **bound to the order**: it looks for cover from **both** enemies **inside the order's area first** (14 u around the ordered point; a unit still travelling has no area yet and skips that tier), then the nearest such cover anywhere, sprints there like Defense (down to the 8-boost floor, no arming), paces once hidden, and ends with the reload — after which the order resumes as after any reflex (travel replans, an anchored unit walks back to its ring; the 40 s hold clock keeps running). No cover anywhere: the reload is spent on the order's legs and Cover Hide is not retried for 0.5 s. No badge, the move order stands throughout, and a new move order landing mid-reload takes over at once. **A fresh hit under a move order runs plain Defense** — the hit-triggered Cover Hide measured about half of Defense's survival time in bot duels on Factory, Streets and Airport (the hidden unit paces at walk speed, stops firing back, and two routing enemies re-acquire it within seconds), so it was retired on 2026-09-27.
- **Reflexes always preempt orders**: Defense escapes, the anti-sniper dodge, the reload hide, hit-stun and the rest interrupt exactly as in autonomous play, and the route replans from wherever the unit ends up.

**Commanded travel dashes on the normal stamina economy** — no speed cheat. Dash segments are *latched*: a segment only starts once boost reaches **125** (or the unit's cap if lower), spends down to a **50-boost reserve floor**, then the unit walks until the gauge re-arms. Route jumps fund at the flat **mandated tier — 60 boost** (2026-08-22, shared with the Defense survival hop; the bot's discretionary jumps fund from its 200 travel floor since 2026-09-29 — 250 before — which the 50↔125 dash cycle could never reach), and while a jump lies ahead on the route the dash floor rises to **70** so the unit always arrives at the ledge able to afford the hop. Cap, drain and regen are the human values throughout; combat reflexes keep their own funding rules (Defense may still spend through the floor to survive).

**Status icons** — gold, on both the unit's marker and its info card: **`!`** while a move order stands (en route plus the 40 s anchor), an **eye** while force-locking (pairing with the colored triangles on the enemy); both side by side when both are active, a **shield** while hiding (which by construction has neither; on the marker it sits centred above the square, or above the diamond's top point), no icon when the unit is autonomous. On the info card the icons are 14×12 px (11×9.5 compact) since 2026-09-26 — the 20×17 originals hung below the card frame once the portrait grew the name row.

**Camera & HUD** — free camera: drag empty ground to pan, pinch / wheel to zoom, `Q`/`E` / right-mouse drag / two-finger twist to rotate about the screen centre; the tilt-shift blur keeps the miniature look with a clear pocket around every unit. Info cards dock in fixed corners — your team bottom-left, enemies bottom-right — each carrying HP and a **stamina bar** (the enemy's stamina bar is hidden online). The joystick, action buttons and the bottom-center stamina bar are gone: fire / dodge / jump / sprint are bot decisions, and each unit's stamina lives on its info card. On Factory, Lobby, Station, Airport and Flashpoint the tall airborne dressing (pipes, trusses, signage, ceiling grids) hides while command mode stands and returns in classic — visual only, nothing collidable. **Lighting is the map's own** (b1027b, user 2026-10-30, "the map looks yellowish in command mode"): the diorama used to re-light every map in a warm daylight grade (Lobby and Airport excepted since 2.1g), which turned grey streets and walls yellowish; command mode now keeps each map's normal lights and sets only its pale background, the fog push-out and the camera planes.

### Command mode online

- **Queue-room pick, hidden from opponents.** The Shooter / Command chip sits above the roster in the waiting room; the pick locks when the match starts (no mid-match toggle online) and is never sent to the other team — only teammates see it, as the gold **[CMD]** roster tag. Behavior will still hint it; accepted.
- **Your unit — and your bot teammate (2026-08-22).** A commander drives their own unit and, when the teammate slot was **bot-filled at match start**, that bot too (same tap/drag orders; the bot's lock triangles wear its own slot color; each unit has its own 0.5 s order rate limit). Human teammates are never commandable, and the commandable set freezes at match start — a mid-match disconnect turns that human's unit into a plain bot, but it is not adopted.
- **Server-authoritative orders.** The commander's client sends dedicated order messages; the server re-validates each one against the same pathfind checks (with a 500 ms rate limit per ordered unit — your own unit and the bot teammate throttle separately, and clears are never rate-limited) and answers with an ack the selection glow waits for. Commander clients skip movement prediction entirely — the unit is server-driven like any bot, and command timers run on the server clock.
- **Teammate share (2v2).** A command teammate sees your full annotations (destination ring, icons, lock triangles) in their own diorama. A **classic** teammate gets an in-world render: a ground ring in the commander's slot color standing at your unit's ordered destination, plus your lock triangles framing just outside their own crosshair when they face the pinned enemy. Both indicators die with the commander's unit.
- **Information hiding.** Snapshots are filtered per team: the enemy's boost value, standing orders and bot-intent state never reach your client. Boost-inference tells (overheat, sprint-lock, thruster effects) deliberately stay visible. Spectators watch in classic view with no command overlays.
- **Disconnects.** 1v1 keeps the instant forfeit; in 2v2 a disconnected commander's orders clear and the unit fights on as a plain bot.

**Reading the stun column** (`duration @ move-scale`): every landed hit slows the victim's movement to *move-scale* for *duration* — e.g. `100 ms @ 0.25` means crawling at 25% speed for 100 ms. Each new hit refreshes it; when two stuns compete, the heavier slow (lower scale) wins. **In practice the slow itself is a minor stat**: sprinting pays straight through it (and everyone sprints away from fire anyway, stun or not), while a walking target was already highly hittable — so the currencies that actually decide fights are the damage actually landed and the boost the target burns escaping, not the movement penalty.

**Reading the spread columns (2026-09-22, spread bloom — ported from the demo line, owner questionnaire):** every cone angle is in radians and describes the FULL cone — a shot deviates from the aim line by at most half of it. Horizontal spread (HA) is gone from every unit. Instead each shot **blooms** the cone: firing adds *Bloom / shot* to the gun's spread, up to *SA cap*, and once the *Recovery* delay has passed since the last shot the extra spread drains back at the listed rate (the two 180 RPM rifles recover between the shots of a burst too — *no delay* — which is what shapes them: Fubuki's or Aris's second quick shot leaves at 0.063 rather than 0.02). Since angular error grows with distance, a cone has a **sure-hit distance** against a standing target — 3.2 ÷ spread, 3.2 being the hit capsule's width — inside which every shot lands; beyond it, hit chance falls off roughly as sure-hit ÷ distance. The table gives it at the base cone and at the cap: a fresh trigger pull is pin-point, a long spray is not, and tapping keeps a gun near its base numbers. **Standing still is the other way to stay pin-point**: a shooter that has stood on the ground at under 1 unit/s for 200 ms fires without adding bloom, and whatever bloom it carried drains at the gun's recovery rate even while it keeps firing — the price is being a stationary target. The shotguns ignore the cones entirely: their pellets fly a fixed 8-point pattern that opens toward ~5.8 wide over the first 70 units of flight — at lock range it is still a tight ~3.3-wide cluster; Hoshino's pattern is additionally stretched 1.4× horizontally (details below).

**Preferred engage distance:** every unit's fighting range is its **lock range ± 7** — the band where bots hold position, orbit, and fire (in 1v1: shotgun 33–47, SMGs 43–57, snipers 113–127). One rule for all weapons: retune a lock range and the combat distance follows. **In 2v2 every bot switches to its 2v2 lock value** (the second number in the weapons table): the team's fighting bands compress into 50–70 so long-lock units stop hanging back — and letting a teammate die alone at the front — while short-lock units step up slightly. 1v1 keeps the classic values, and the change is bot-behavior only: the player-side lock reticle always reads the 1v1 number. Every pickable unit carries a 2v2 value; a unit without one would simply fall back to its 1v1 lock range.

**Measured hit rates** (2026-09-26 — remeasured for the spread bloom; the pre-bloom Shooting Range lanes are retired; remeasured 2026-10-24 for the four units added since and the day's recovery changes — the harness is `shared/tools/hitcurve.mjs`, the chart `shared/tools/hitcurve_png.py`, both in the tree now; the 0.8.4 script was never committed). Once a cone grows with every round, one percentage no longer describes a gun: what matters is **how the hit chance decays across a magazine while the shooter is on the move**. So the new protocol is a walking full-magazine spray, run on the shared sim (the code the server runs) on Plain Field, 200 runs per series:

- **Shooter:** strafes sideways at her own walk speed (16 or 12 u/s; Koyuki 10, Hina 8), reversing every 400 ms so she is never still and every round blooms, and holds the trigger through **one whole magazine** (tap-fired for the shotguns and snipers; a charging sniper is rooted by the sim, as in play), **obeying the bot fire gate** (`botMayFire`): only the marksman rifles' line — the 100% sure-hit line — ever closes inside their own lock range, so Fubuki and Aris fire one round per 0.6 s from a fully recovered cone, exactly as their bots do; every other gun's line sits beyond its lock range even at the cap, and the gate never trips. Every unit fires from **her own lock range** only.
- **Target:** a real unit — the 3.2-wide hit capsule, not the Range's signs — ping-ponging on a ±35° arc at lock range around the shooter, so it always moves across the line of fire, at three speeds: standing, the shooter's own walk speed, and that walk speed + 11.76 (the sprint boost). Reversal at the arc ends is instant, so the speed never dips. Shots aim at the target's current position, exactly as the game does (no lead).
- **Scoring:** every round is a hit or a miss (shotguns: pellets landed ÷ 8 per blast; Kei's beam counts as a round). Each curve is the hit chance of the *n*-th round of the magazine, averaged over the 200 runs; the numbers in the table are the mean over the whole magazine and, in brackets, the mean of its first third → last third.

### Aris (Unit 7) — flight & laser bolts

- **Flight kit**: a jump tap in the air re-fires the jump impulse (12 boost per pop, no cooldown); *holding* jump sustains a climb at sprint speed; air-sprint flies **level** (dedicated fly art); the air-dodge holds altitude. Boost does not regen while airborne — altitude is a spent resource.
- Sprinting into a jump **carries the sprint momentum** through the air.
- Her shot is a **64-unit-long laser bolt**: the thin cyan cylinder you see *is* the hitbox (both derive from one spec entry). It grows out of the muzzle — the body never reaches behind the spawn point — and hits with its whole length, so a dodge must clear the entire passing beam, not just its nose.

### Sniper charge & sprint-cancel

- Both snipers hold their shot on a **1 s charge** (locked in place). Holding sprint cancels the charge and fires early — but never before a **0.5 s floor** (costs ½ a dodge's boost). So the shooter picks any release point in the **0.5–1 s** window, and the target always gets at least that much glint-to-bullet warning.
- **Online floating unlock:** against a human defender the 0.5 s floor counts from the moment their client *actually rendered* the glint — the defender's client acks the glint's first frame and the server slides the earliest release to that ack + 0.5 s — the defender's half-second of SEEN warning is absolute. If the ack never arrives (defender's tab backgrounded, client stalled; the server waits up to 0.5 s), the earliest release becomes press + 1.0 s — exactly a normal full charge, so the attacker's worst case is simply losing the fast cancel for that shot. Offline play and bot defenders (who see server truth instantly) are unchanged.

**Sniper timing at a glance** (worked example: defender's network delay 0.05 s each way; projectile flight and 16 ms tick rounding excluded). Column definitions — *release time*: server clock, from processing the attacker's FIRE input to creating the projectile; *glint duration*: on each player's own display, from the frame the glint is first drawn to the frame the shot is drawn; *read & decide*: on the defender's side, from the glint's first drawn frame to the latest DODGE press that still reaches the server before the projectile exists.

| Release time | Glint duration (attacker's screen) | Glint duration (defender's screen) | Read & decide time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.60 (earliest the server permits) | 0.60 | 0.60 | **0.50** |
| 0.70 | 0.70 | 0.70 | 0.60 |
| 0.80 | 0.80 | 0.80 | 0.70 |
| 0.90 | 0.90 | 0.90 | 0.80 |
| 1.00 (server auto-fires) | 1.00 | 1.00 | **0.90** |

Three properties the table encodes: glint duration equals the release time on **every** screen (both endpoints of the interval shift by the same delivery delay, so its length is preserved for any observer); the defender's read & decide time is always the release time minus their round trip (one delivery lost at each end); and the earliest-release fence sits at ack + 0.5 s precisely so the read & decide column can never fall below 0.50 — the guarantee is produced by *placing the fence*, not by adjusting any clock. The full charge is the one release with no fence involvement: it fires at press + 1.0 s flat, so its read & decide time shrinks with the defender's round trip (lag-taxed like every ordinary attack), while the fast cancel's 0.50 is lag-proof.
- The **dodge** is the counter: a step grants **0.3 s** of i-frame immunity, so a well-timed dodge passes through the shot. Aru's bullet speed is **4000 u/s** (user 2026-10-22, 2500 before: near-hitscan — 0.03 s of flight even at max range, two ticks); Kei's beam is instant.

### Aru (Unit 3) — range zones & the lock reticle

- Damage is tiered by distance, **locked at fire time**: under 15 units → **20**, 15–50 → **35**, beyond 50 → **50**. Rushing a sniper is real counterplay; long range stays lethal.
- The lock reticle shows the current zone — plain brackets (<15), **+ cross ticks** (15–50), **+ inner bars** (50+). It appears both when *you* play Aru (your tier on the target) and when your lock target *is* an Aru (which of her zones you're standing in).
- The reticle turns **red** not just when your target fires, but for the whole time a sniper (Aru **or** Kei) is **mid-charge with you as the target** — a continuous danger signal from glint to shot. Kei's live sweep channel also holds it red for the whole channel, whoever it is aimed at — the beam can hit anyone.

### Kei (Unit 6) — Railgun (照射ビーム beam)

- Fires an instant **hitscan beam** (30 damage, one hit per enemy per beam, blocked by walls, ~0.5 s fade) instead of a bullet. The beam also **deletes projectiles** it touches. The hit volume stops one combined body-width (beam radius + target radius) short of the wall it ends on (2026-09-10), so neither the quick beam nor the sweep channel registers on a unit standing just behind cover — before, the end cap reached up to ~3.2 u (channel ~4 u) through the wall.
- Holding the charge to the full **1 s** fires a **sweep channel**: a 1 s locked, steerable beam (1.5× width, **20 damage**, one hit per enemy for the whole channel). The stick steers it — horizontal and vertical — at ~10°/s; sprint cancels the channel. The fire cooldown is paused during the channel and starts when it ends.
- Her glint grows toward **2×** size as the charge fills, telegraphing a full-charge sweep.

**Bots vs. the sniper.**
- **As the shooter:** both sniper bots flip a **50/50 coin** per shot — release at the **0.5 s floor** (a fast snap; for Kei, the quick beam) or hold to the **full 1 s charge** (for Kei, the sweep channel). No in-between releases.
- **On defense:** when a glint aimed at it appears — from **any** enemy, locked or not (mirroring the human's edge-indicator awareness; earliest active charge wins) — the bot **rolls its reaction per charge**, and the slow roll is charger-aware:
  - **Anti-Aru** (bullet snipers): **50% at 0.4 s** (i-frames open ahead of the earliest possible cancel, covering **every floor snap at any range** — but a full hold sails in after they end) / **50% at 0.8 s** (deliberately late: a snap lands first and cancels the pending dodge, but the i-frames ~0.8–1.1 s sit exactly on the **full hold's** impact). Against the shooter's 50/50 snap/hold flip neither side can be read; equilibrium **~50% of charges convert** (snaps beat slow rolls, holds beat fast rolls).
  - **Anti-Kei** (beam snipers): **50% at 0.4 s** (covers the instant quick beam) / **50% at 0.9 s** — the dodge starts just ahead of the **sweep channel's** aimed opening (i-frames ~0.9–1.2 s blanket it) and the follow-up sprint outruns the beam's steering at normal fighting ranges. (An 0.8 s roll would be dead weight here: the quick beam pre-empts it and the sweep outlives it.)

  Either way it's one dodge (0.3 s i-frames) plus a **0.52 s** committed sprint, both perpendicular to that sniper's line of fire; lock and return fire stay on the current target throughout. Mid-charge hits still cancel a pending dodge, and a cooldown- or boost-blocked defender still eats the shot. After the committed sprint expires the bot has no awareness of a still-live sweep — it can wander back into the channel.

**Bot trigger discipline.** A bot fires in continuous bursts of a fixed per-unit length (`botFireCap` — the "fire cap"), resting ~0.8–1.5 s between bursts. This rhythm is the bottom layer; the **bloom gate** (Bot logic below, 2026-09-22) sits on top of it and only shapes the machine guns' and the marksman rifles' fire inside their bands. Shots inside a burst pace at the weapon's own RPM-derived cooldown, so retuning a fire rate retunes the bot with it. Every auto's cap equals its **full magazine** — an auto bot fires until the mag runs dry and rolls straight into the reload; the shotguns are burst-gated at 4 blasts per pull; the snipers run their charge cycle instead of bursting.

## Bot logic

One bot brain drives every bot — all maps, all modes, offline and online (one module since 2026-10-17, `shared/src/sim/nbrain.js`: the server's `tickBot` and the offline client's `updateEnemy` each build a small host and call `nbBrainTick` once a tick, exactly as the Sudden Death brain is called; every bot slot in 1v1 / 2v2 / Trio / spectator uses the identical body). In **Sudden Death** the cover brain described in that section replaces Pursue / Engage; everything below is the Health Point brain.

**State machine** — `Defense > Maze > Engage > Pursue`, re-evaluated every tick:

- **Pursue** — closes to (or backs off to) the fighting band, **lock range ± 7**. Sprints in **latched legs** (2026-09-29): a leg arms only from a **full tank (250)** and runs down to **200**, then the bot walks until the tank is full again — roughly 0.7 s of sprint per 0.7 s of walk, and Maze routing shares the latch. (Both bounds used to sit on the 250 reserve: in Pursue the release won at exactly 250, so it never sprinted; in Maze the dispatch floor let one dash tick through every half second — the flickering sprint pose.) Elevation aids: jump up toward a higher target when close, hop off ledges toward a lower one, and on low ground hop onto any mountable ledge it brushes within jump reach.
- **Engage** — the in-band fight: orbits the target with a range correction toward the sweet spot, flips orbit direction to stay inside sight, and runs the peek-cover rhythm (tuck behind cover while the weapon cycles, drift out exactly when it's ready to fire). Two consecutive ticks of driving into a wall also flip the orbit — the tangent is perpendicular to the aim line, so reversing it always points away from the face just hit, and the bot unsticks itself inside the fight instead of breaking off to re-route. Opportunistically hops onto reachable ledges — high ground is the better vantage.
- **Defense** — triggered by a fresh hit (read off `lastHitAt`, which every landed hit stamps whether or not it stuns — until 2026-10-23 the brain read the stun window rising, so a `stunImmune` unit's bot never knew it was hit, and a hit landing inside a running stun of the same weight went unseen; the references were re-frozen at b1023n): a committed straight cover-sprint (may spend boost to the hard floor — survival overrides the reserve), then a tuck-and-peek once cover breaks line of sight. A jumpable ledge lying **dead ahead** on the committed escape line (within ~37°) is hopped without breaking stride — never while a sniper dodge is scheduled (airborne can't dodge), and funded at the flat **mandated-jump tier (60 boost**, 2026-08-22 — shared with command-mode route jumps; discretionary travel jumps fund from the 200 travel floor since 2026-09-29, 250 before**)**. If the escape wedges anyway, a wider-angle vault, then a direction flip.
- **Maze** — the router. Asks the nav grid for a real walk route (waypoint-followed, cut at the first spot that can already fire) and retries it on every stall signal; when no route exists it keeps moving on a minimal steer biased away from the last spot it got pinned at (a short back-out frees a truly wedged body first). Entered **proactively**: the moment the straight walk toward an out-of-band target is blocked (instantly when sightless; after 0.25 s when the target is visible over a low obstacle), plus the classic stuck detectors (a 1 s wedged/spinning window, a 1.5 s no-progress clock) and a 2 s no-line-of-sight clock. "Wedged" is judged on **net** movement — under 1.7 units in a second is stuck no matter how much wall the bot rubbed getting nowhere, which is what catches a slow slide along a face rather than only a dead stop.

**Cover Defense — measured and shelved (2026-09-27).** The owner asked whether Defense should take cover into account: a *Cover Defense* that, on a fresh hit, picks a spot hidden from **both** enemies (8 headings × 6 / 12 u rings, straight-walkable, scored cover-from-both > cover-from-whoever-sees-me-now > any, plus crossing the aim line; reach budgeted by boost — 80+ for the 12 u ring, 45+ for 6 u), sprints there committed until arrival (cap 1.2 s, no 400 ms re-perpendicularization on the way), counts as *in cover* only when hidden from every live enemy, keeps Defense's tuck-and-peek, re-picks when the second enemy regains sight and lands a hit, and spends to the same 8-boost floor. It was built on a scratchpad copy of the shared sim and measured against plain Defense in 2v2 bot duels on Factory, Streets and Airport (40 runs per cell, 45 s cap, Saori and Koyuki as the observed unit, both under a standing move order and in full autonomy): **worse in every cell** — mean survival 30.1 → 22.4 s, survived 38% → 12%, wins 38% → 11%, damage dealt down a fifth (enemy HP lost 156 → 125). The mechanism itself worked (most picks were double-cover spots, over 90% reached, no run hit the cap), so the loss is the behaviour: the unit takes about the same 16 hits per life, only faster — the straight run to cover is more predictable than Defense's 400 ms re-perpendicularized strafe, the 300 ms tuck is a standing target, the run points the gun away or out of sight, and two routing enemies turn a re-used cover spot into a flank. Same verdict as the hit-triggered Cover Hide (see Command mode): in this sim, sprinting across the aim line while firing back beats running to cover. Not adopted — nothing shipped, Defense unchanged, the Cover Hide plan left as it stands. Two variants were left untried should it be revisited: the 6 u ring only, and no tuck (peek and fire on arrival).

**Steering** — each tick, a moving bot's direction is the sum of two pulls: "toward where it's going" plus "away from any wall it's about to touch"; the second pull is what makes bots slide around obstacles instead of walking into them. Invisible unit-only fences (the kind bullets fly through) get sorted by height: a fence low enough to jump (Station's / Flashpoint's 4-high platform edges) gives **no push at all**, so the bot can walk right up to it and the hop is fired by the jump reflexes above; a fence too tall to jump (Square's 14-high fountain colonnade, the tall panes flanking Streets' bridge slopes) pushes like any solid wall. And a fence the bot is already standing **above** pushes nothing — it passes over freely.

**What counts as a ledge, and when a hop goes** (user 2026-10-24; one test shared by every perch behaviour): the top has to sit **1.7–4.8** above the bot's floor — lower is walked onto, higher needs a ramp — the lip must be unfenced, and the top must be at least **6 wide** (a body and a half; a jump carries 12–17 units horizontally, so anything thinner gets sailed clean over instead of mounted). Then the hop asks three questions, in every state: is the ledge **the way I am going** (its direction within ~60° of this tick's move — the route's next leg in Maze, the pursuit or orbit line otherwise), **has the enemy not fired at me in the last second** (a body in the air cannot dodge, so a hop under fire hands over a round), and — when the trigger is an enemy standing a jump higher — **is a ledge actually within reach** (1.1 u), not a blind jump at a belt ten units off. The Maze's width-0 exemption of 2026-08-10 (a strip too thin to be a vantage was still a legitimate thing to climb — bought with one Factory belt measurement) is withdrawn: on Warehouse it read the 0.25 m lips of the containers as steps. The Defense **vault** — pressed against a ledge with nowhere else to go — keeps its own gates; it is the way out, not a vantage, and jumping *down* off high ground is untouched. Measured on the shared sim (12 bot-vs-bot matches a map, 60 s each): hops a bot-minute Warehouse 18.5 → 7.5, Station 57 → 24, Town 38 → 19, Factory 2 8.3 → 6.5, Dust 13 → 13.5 (its jumps are descents); same-floor bounces — a hop that landed where it started — Warehouse 42 → 6, Town 82 → 5; Station's 4 m platforms are still climbed (131 → 57 climbs in the sample, the bounces gone). The three questions are three comparisons on numbers the brain already holds: no new scan, the brain's cost is unchanged.

**Sight** — a bot "sees" its target only along lines a bullet could actually fly: the sight ray is blocked by every solid obstacle, and by walkable **ramps and elevated decks** too — a ramp is solid fill, so nothing sees through the wedge; a bridge deck on open pillars blocks only rays that cross the deck plane, so two units both *under* the bridge still see each other. The eye sits **1.6 above the body centre** (`BOT_LOS_EYE_HEIGHT`) on both hosts; until 2026-10-17 the offline client read it from the root + 1.6, 2.35 higher than online, so offline bots saw over cover that online bots did not.

**Seeing vs shooting** — those are two separate questions, asked from two different heights. *Sight* is the eye line, and it drives awareness: target choice, routing, cover, when to break off. *Firing* is gated on the **muzzle** line instead — the exact line the bullet will take, tested with the bullet's own rules. The two are not interchangeable: a bullet leaves the chest, not the eye, so anything sitting in the gap between them (a slope's guard rail, a deck's underside, the slope surface itself) used to let a bot hold a picture-perfect sight line into a shot that died on the geometry. The gate now *is* the shot test, so a bot that pulls the trigger always has a path — and it will also take a shot through an invisible unit-only fence, because bullets genuinely pass through those. Sight treats such a fence as opaque only where it is flagged `blocksBotSight` (Streets' under-slope bars and slope gates — see-through walls the bot must route around); every other unit-only fence is as transparent to sight as it is to bullets.

**Sniper play** — covered in the sniper section above: 50/50 snap-or-hold as the shooter; as the defender, a per-charge dodge roll timed against the charger's kit, one committed perpendicular dodge + sprint.

**Bloom gate and suppressing fire** (ported from the demo line 2026-09-22) — a bot fires freely only while its target sits inside the gun's *current* **gate line**, re-checked every tick from the live distance: one closed-form division, run before the obstacle scan so an out-of-range poll costs nothing. The line is a fixed width divided by the bloomed cone: for the **auto weapons it is 8.84 ÷ cone — the distance where the cone still lands one round in three on a standing target** (the 3.2 × 6.4 capsule is a third of the cone's disc at radius 4.42; 2.76× the sure-hit line); for the **marksman rifles (Fubuki, Aris) it is the sure-hit line itself, 3.2 ÷ cone** (`botGateWidth: 3.2`); **the machine guns (Koyuki, Hina) have no line at all** (`botGateWidth: 0`, owner 2026-09-25 — a 0% threshold in both modes: they spray on the burst / rest rhythm alone, and for them a burst is the whole magazine — `botFireCap` equals the mag for every auto — so the reload is the rest). At their caps the other autos' lines sit at 147 (Saori, Asuna), 110 (Atsuko) and 80 (Marina), so inside the rifles' and SMGs' 43–63 bands the gate never trips and what you see there is the plain **burst / rest rhythm** of the trigger discipline above. When a line does close on a target (an auto pushed out past its capped line), the bot **holds until the cone has fully recovered** (200 ms + the bloom ÷ recovery) — the line drifting back out past a standing target does not release the hold, so there is no one-round trickle — and opens up again from the base cone. A target that **closes in** past where the line stood when the hold began releases it at once. Beyond the base cone's line (442 for the 0.02 guns, 295 for Atsuko, 221 for Hina, 147 for Marina) a recovered auto fires a committed **suppress burst** (five rounds for the rifles and machine guns, ten for the SMGs) and repeats. The **marksman rifles** live on the gate: each shot adds 0.10 of bloom and 0.17/s takes 0.59 s to shed it, so at their band they settle at one shot per ~0.6 s; their suppress burst is a single round (`botSuppressBurst: 1`). Shotguns (fixed pattern) and the snipers (their cone out-reaches lock range) are never gated. Offline and online run the same rule (shared `bloom.js`, `botMayFire` / `botGateDistance`). The demo line measured the gate line's effect over 6,336 bot duels (see that branch's README); this line has not been re-measured.

**Bot fire-gate thresholds** — the hit rate on a standing target below which the bot stops firing (the line is *width ÷ current cone*; 33% = width 8.84, 100% = the 3.2 sure-hit width, 0% = no line):

| Unit | 1v1 | 2v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Saori, Asuna, Atsuko, Marina | 33% | 33% |
| Koyuki, Hina | 0% (no gate) | 0% (no gate) |
| Fubuki, Aris | 100% | 100% |
| Hoshino, Haruka, Aru, Kei | never gated | never gated |

**Reload hide** (the 2026-08-08 cover reload, re-based on the Hide stance 2026-09-26) — weapons with a manual reload of 3 s or more (Hina's 7 s drum, Koyuki's 5 s) spend it in the **Hide** stance: the unit enters Hide the tick the reload starts and leaves it the tick the reload completes, with every Hide rule — out of every enemy's sight, pacing its cover, slipping away from a closing enemy, no Defense on a hit, firing back only from where it stands. Every bot does it (enemies too); on your own units in command mode the shield badge shows, entering **wipes a standing force lock**, and a new order during the reload releases the hide for the rest of that reload, exactly as it would any Hide (a Hide you set yourself is not released by the reload ending). **A unit under a move order keeps it**: its reload runs as a *Cover Hide* bound to the order instead (2026-09-26 — cover inside the order's area first, for the whole reload, no badge), and the unit resumes its post when the reload completes; a new move order landing mid-reload takes over at once. Per-shell and auto reloaders (Hoshino, Haruka, Aris) are excluded by design — they're always mid-reload, so they'd never come out. The counterplay is to push: hiding beats a stationary opponent, not a committed one.

**Station's hops, and Factory's belts** (b1024z10–z11, user 2026-10-24) — Station: a bot that had left a platform — walked off its edge while orbiting, or jumped down to close on the enemy — hopped straight back onto it, the platform being the strong ground the low-ground rule reached for, and landed at whatever range that gave. Gone on the user's call (`nbrain.js`): the **low-ground hop** — "on low ground, take any reachable platform, no 'toward player' gate; on Station the decks are the strong positions" — in Pursue, Engage and Defense alike; and with it two things tried the same day and dropped the same day: a fourth question for a hop ("did I not just come down from there?", a 4 s refusal of a hop back to the floor just left) and a gate on the pursuit's deliberate drop (only when the enemy stands lower) — the question read heights, not places, so it also kept a body backing off from hopping onto the other platform, and a hop that keeps the range must stay. What remains climbs for a reason: the pursuit's climb toward an enemy a jump higher, the Maze's hop onto a ledge in its way, a route's vault at its link, Defense's sprint vault and its vault out of a wedge. Measured over six bot-minutes of 1v1: a body left a platform once or twice a minute (six or seven before) and never came straight back up the same side (five or six times before); it spends 9–29 % of a match on a platform, where it was 30–80 % — the platforms are climbed by routes and the pursuit only. And the wedge detectors read the sim's own note of a wall (b1024z12, user 2026-10-24: "the bot got stuck on the railway under fire"): Defense and Engage judged "I am pressed against a wall" from the steering's boxes alone, and the steering leaves jumpable fences out on purpose — Station's 1 m platform fronts among them — so a body pressed against a front stood still, its vault out of a wedge asleep, once the low-ground hop that used to lift it was gone (measured: 1.6–3.3 s a minute still under fire on the tracks, 71–90 % of a match down there). Now moving into the face physics pushed the body out of last tick (`host.wall`, `pushedInto` in `nbrain.js`) counts as wedged too, whatever the thing; Pursue's wedge was always read from movement and needed nothing. Measured after: 0.0–0.3 s a minute still under fire, 7–25 % of a match on the tracks, the platforms climbed again through the vault out of the wedge — so the quick return up the side a body fled from is back, two to five a minute, now as the escape's way out rather than an idle hop. Every jump a bot takes is tagged with its rule (`botHopWhy`: pursue-climb, pursue-descent, pursue-low, maze-perch, maze-route, low-ground, defense-ahead, defense-vault, cover-move) for the headless harness to read. Factory: no bot ever crossed a belt, by two faults of the nav grid (`navgrid.js`). A walk edge's fit at the crossing's midpoint was read at the floor the walk stands on there — past the step up onto the belt top (2.6 units, a step's height) — so the 2.9-unit lips before the step were never met, the belt read as walked onto, and the sim's lips then stopped the body; and a jump link between two walk components needed a rise of at least `JUMP_RISE_MIN`, which the belt's 2.6 falls short of. Now every walk edge is also swept at the lower floor's body height (`walkSegmentBlocked`), and an island link needs no least rise (the components are apart, so no walk joins them; a shortcut within one component keeps it). Factory gains 48 links, ground to belt top, and the bots vault its belts in the headless matches (about one crossing per bot-minute); elsewhere one walkable node changes (Town) and four routes grow by 2 m where a low thing really blocks the walk. The two equivalence tests' references are re-frozen at b1024z12 (`shared/tools/freeze_refs.py`).

**Where a perch is looked for (b1024z15).** The plain brain's two floor searches, `findHighGroundPerch` and the first loop of `findDescentDirection`, used to walk every floor of the map, up to five times a tick per bot; on Compound that is 16,878 floors, and it was the brains' largest cost there. They now ask the sim's point broadphase (`obstaclesNearPoint`) for the floors within the seek radius and test only those. The broadphase hands back a superset of those floors in the list's own order, so the same floor wins as before: the frozen-brain tests pass unchanged against the b1024z12 refs. In the headless harness Compound's mean bot cost fell from 0.65 to 0.29 ms per tick; the spikes, which are grid searches over 20k nodes, are untouched.

**The firing-position search's cost (b1025c, user 2026-10-25: the online lag on Compound).** Maze's first choice, the search for the nearest spot that sees the target from inside the band, was the brains' largest single cost on the big maps: it grew from the bot's feet in every direction with no destination (Dijkstra), and every candidate it reached was tested for sight against every box and every floor of the map — on Compound 22,666 and 16,878 — at two eye heights; three bots could run it in the same tick, and the server's 6 ms search budget covered only the hide search. Four rules now, in `nbrain.js` navPlan and `navgrid.js` findFiringPath: the sight test reads the broadphase like every other sight test (identical answers); the expansion is directed (A*: the key is the walk so far plus a lower bound of the walk still needed to reach the band — the straight-line distance to the band's edge in cells, over 1.5, so it never exceeds the true cost and the spot found is still a cheapest one; 300 random pairs per map: every spot that differed from the old search's tied it exactly in walking cost); the search gets the tick's slice (`host.deadline`) and parks when it runs out, continuing next tick from the same open set while the bot follows an interim route to the target (the walk path, cut at the first spot that fires); one route search per tick per match (`host.searchSlot`, the hide search's turn rule); and a bot farther than `BOT_FIRING_SEARCH_REACH` (2.5) times its band's upper edge walks first and searches only inside the reach — a shotgun beyond 117 u, an SMG beyond 142 u, while a sniper's reach is 317 u, so it keeps crossing the map to its lane. `smoothPath`'s per-chord prefilter, which walked every box of the map, is gone too (the overlap test reads the point index itself). Headless, Compound, four shotgun bots: 0.43 → 0.22 ms per tick, ticks over 16 ms 19 → 5 a minute; the 1v1 harness's worst tick 113 → 22 ms. The references were re-frozen at b1025c (the turn and reach rules change the bots' timing). **The walk route too (b1025e, user 2026-10-25: "the respawn lag is the far route").** The spikes left after b1025c all fell on ticks where a bot stood 200–470 u from its target — after a respawn, typically — and none within half a second of a death or a respawn itself: the walk route across the map (`findPathOnGrid`, the interim and fallback route) still ran whole in one tick. It is resumable now like the firing search (its job on the route record, the tick's slice, parked before a pop so the resumed search is the unbudgeted one step for step — `shared/test/route_budget.test.js`), a call always makes 32 pops of progress so a deadline already past cannot stall it, and navPlan runs one search a tick: the walk route first while the bot has no route at all, the firing search next, its spot replacing the interim route; until any route lands the Maze leg's fallback walks the bot toward the target. A plan still searching when the bot arrives at its route's end is kept rather than dropped. Headless Trio 2v2 with respawns on Compound, 90 s: ticks over 16 ms 7 → 2, the worst 49 → 19 ms, none within half a second of a death or respawn; the 1v1 harness's worst tick 22 ms; the four-shotgun match fires as before (196 shots). References re-frozen at b1025e. **The budget itself (b1025f, user 2026-10-25: "smoothness over the bots' route"):** the server's slice is 2 ms (6 before): with the searches resumable the slice is spent nearly every tick while any bot's search is pending, and on the free instance's tenth of a core 6 ms of wall clock was a third of the tick for a tenth of a fast core's progress — the tick ran late every time and the players' prediction snapped back (the "backtrace" report). A tick that starts half a tick late grants no search at all (`matchState.searchClosed`, `LATE_TICK_GAP_MS`), the bots' order rotates each tick so the one search a tick is shared instead of hogged by the first bot, and the server logs its tick cost and late starts every 30 s (`[tick] 30 s: …`). A cross-map route on Compound is ~90 ms of work on that core, ~45 ticks at 2 ms, ~0.7 s; a firing search from far ~0.85 s; three bots in turn stretch the last one's to about 2 s. The bot walks toward its target meanwhile, and a hit still puts it in Defense: the state machine runs every tick, the route record waits.

**The slide** (b1026k, 2026-10-29; found measuring Town's cost after the lips of b1026j) — the Sudden Death follower (`sdbrain.js`, THE ROUTE) dropped a route the moment the heading to the next waypoint pointed into the wall the body touched, marked the waypoint and searched again. Where the route's first square lay across a thin post's line — Town's stair balusters, a body pressed on one as it came onto the stairs from the side, the way the lips' new links sent it — the search returned the same route, dropped the same tick, every tick to the end of the match: 2,000 searches, 6.5 ms of sim a tick, the body never moving. A heading that only grazes the wall now goes on along it — the heading less its part into the wall, what the push-out leaves of it anyway — with the route kept; the route is dropped as before head-on (less than `SD.slideMin` of the heading, sin 20°, left along the wall) or when the slide at one waypoint has gone on for `SD.slideTicks` ticks (45, 0.7 s). Town's match after: 1.3 ms a tick and 100 searches (6.5 and 2,000 before); District's 1.6 with the slide on or off. The bots' equivalence tests are re-frozen at b1026k: play differs wherever a body brushed a wall on a route.

**Map-agnostic by design.** The bot rules that shipped carry no per-map special cases; a set of Station-only behaviors was built and field-tested, then deleted once repositioning Station's spawns solved the same problem more cleanly.

Double-tap `K` (or the sprint button) while holding a movement direction to lock sprint. The lock releases only after the stick/keys stay **neutral for a sustained 0.18 s** — flipping direction through the joystick center (left→right) or swapping movement keys keeps the locked sprint alive; letting go still stops it almost instantly. Dodge (step) grants 0.3 s of damage immunity (i-frames) — the full duration holds even when the dodge runs into a wall (the unit stops at the wall; the animation and i-frames don't cut short).

**Broadphase everywhere** (b1021b–c, i–j) — the plain brain's perch search, every segment and point test against the map's boxes and floors (sight, projectiles, steering), the ground lookups (`groundHeightAt`, `surfaceHeightAtXZ`) and the body's own box tests (`unitOverlapsObstacle`, `resolveUnitObstacleCollisions`, the nav grid's floors at a point) read only the boxes whose cells the query touches. ## Implementation notes

- **Server authoritative.** Shared sim runs on the server at ~62.5 Hz; clients predict their own local fighter and reconcile against snapshots.
- **The input queue (user 2026-10-25, "the backtrace while sprinting").** The server used to keep one frame per player — each arriving frame overwrote the last and was acknowledged on arrival — and apply whatever it held once a tick; two frames arriving within one tick meant a step the client had predicted and the server never took, and the next snapshot pulled the player back by it (0.44 u at a sprint). On a server whose ticks come late and bunched (the free instance's CPU quota is pulsed) that was most frames, most of the time. The frames now queue per player (`shared/src/sim/inputqueue.js`) and the tick takes one, in order, acknowledging only what it took: a late server means a step applied late, not a step lost. Two bounds: past 4 waiting frames a tick takes two, merged (the later frame's movement, every tap of both — a skipped step, but only while the server is that far behind); past 16 the oldest are dropped as taken. An empty tick leaves the last frame standing with its taps cleared, as before; a start hold drains the queues unapplied. The client is unchanged — the acknowledgement means what it always meant, only now it is true.
- **Lag compensation (2026-10-17).** A human's shot is judged in the world their screen showed. Every input frame carries `viewAt`, the `serverTime` of the snapshot the client had drawn when the frame was made; the server turns it into the shooter's lag (0–100 ms, `LAG_COMP_MAX_MS`; 200 for its first day, 100 on the owner's call) and keeps the last 144 ms of every body — position and the two immunity clocks — in `shared/src/sim/history.js`, recorded each tick after the bodies move. A round, pellet, beam or sweep fired with a lag aims at and is tested against the target's body of that many milliseconds ago, over its whole flight; the damage lands on the live body. Bots, offline and older clients carry no `viewAt` and read the live body, unchanged (the Health Point and Sudden Death equivalence tests still pass tick for tick). The cost is the usual one — favor the shooter: a hit can land up to 100 ms after the target reached cover on its own screen, and bots, who see the present, give a laggy human that much. Before this the server judged every shot in its present while the shooter's own round was drawn against the enemy as last seen, one network leg behind, so a round seen to land could do nothing and a round seen to miss could hit. The line as it stood before this change is commit f5c2755 (2026-10-17).
- **Bot AI** has one logical state machine (Defense > Maze > Engage > Pursue) in one module, `shared/src/sim/nbrain.js`. The online server (`tickBot` in `shared/src/sim/ai.js`) and the offline client (`updateEnemy` in `client/src/main.js`) are its two hosts: each supplies the enemy views, the command entry and the physics epilogue, and holds no rule of its own.
- **Universal pathfinder.** Maze is route-first: a nav grid is derived from each map's collision data (4-unit cells, A* plus a firing-position search), with jump-links bridging separated walk islands (e.g. Station's raised platforms) so bots climb instead of grinding walls. The old heuristic wall-following was retired once the grid covered every map; no-route ticks now run a minimal steer with a stuck-memory bias, re-planned on every stall signal. The grid is **multi-layer**: a cell can carry several stacked floors (the Streets bridge deck and the road beneath it are different nodes), so bots route under an overpass instead of treating the whole column as one surface. Cells are graded by how much room a body actually has, and A* pays extra to enter a tight one — a price, never a ban, so a genuinely narrow chokepoint costs more and still gets used. Planned routes are then **smoothed into diagonals** where it's provably safe: a run of grid legs collapses into one straight leg only when a swept corridor test passes. Doorways, ramps, jump-links and clutter alleys fail that test by design and keep their grid legs; open-field staircases become the diagonal you'd expect. Bots hold a per-weapon range band centered on their lock range (sweet spot ±7) — one rule for every weapon; in 2v2 the band derives from the compressed `lockRange2v2` value instead.
- **Bullets vs decks and ramps** (humans and bots alike). A walkable surface stops any round that **crosses** it — a shot fired under a bridge deck stays under it, and one fired into a slope dies on the slope. The test is exact: the segment is clipped to the surface's footprint and its height compared against the surface's at the two clipped ends, which is complete because both vary linearly along the ray. It replaced an 8-sample sign-flip walk that was blind to any crossing falling between samples — and a round covers ~34 units in a single tick at 2000 u/s, so fast shots used to pass straight through the Streets bridge slope and hit whoever stood on it. Surfaces are still judged one at a time over their own footprint, so a level shot passing OVER a sidewalk and UNDER the bridge deck cannot "flip" across two unrelated slabs.
- **Stamina economy** is shared by humans and bots — same cap (250), drain (1.1/tick), regen (4.59/tick), and empty-recovery lockout. On a map with a `boostScale` (District: 5) the cap, the regen and every bot threshold are that many times — see **A map's boost scale** under Maps. Bot decisions layer a **travel sprint latch** on top (2026-09-29): a Pursue / Maze sprint leg **arms only from a full tank (250)** and **runs down to 200**, then the bot walks until the tank is full again; discretionary jumps (the pursue elevation aids, Maze route jumps) fund from the same 200 line, so at least 200 stays banked for the survival reflexes. Until then the arm and the release both sat on the 250 reserve, which in practice meant a one-tick dash every half second in Maze (the flickering sprint pose) and no travel sprint at all in Pursue. Two survival exemptions spend through the reserve — Defense escapes under live fire (down to the hard floor of 8) and the anti-glint dodge (gated only by the unit's raw step cost — 48 by default; step cost/duration/cooldown/distance are per-unit tunable like the jump family). **Mandated jumps** — the Defense hop/vault and command-mode route jumps — fire from a flat **60 boost** (2026-08-22) rather than the reserve; every other jump keeps the 200 gate. The latch is purely a decision threshold; the mechanics underneath stay human-identical.
- **Friendly fire** in 2v2 is off — bullets pass through teammates.
- **Per-team snapshot filtering (2026-08-21).** The server builds each snapshot in two team variants (plus a spectator view) and emits per socket: enemy fighters ship with the boost value redacted and every bot-intent field stripped, and each team's variant carries only its **own** commanders' standing orders for teammate rendering. Command state lives in a side-table off the fighter objects, so it cannot leak into a snapshot by construction.
- **Command layer (shared).** The order validation, latched dash travel, anchor orbit and force-lock rules live once in `shared/src/sim/command.js` with their tunables in `shared/src/sim/constants.js` (`CMD_*`) — the offline client and the server authority read the same source, and the client uses the same pathfind checks for its instant order preview/deny. Orders travel on dedicated messages (`order:move` / `order:lock` / `order:stance` / `order:clear`) that the server re-validates and acks, rate-limited to one per 500 ms per ordered unit (a commander driving a bot teammate can order both units back-to-back); `order:clear` is exempt. Travel is pathfinder-guided end to end (2026-08-22): the route re-plans from the current position at every reflex exit and on a 1.5 s cadence (each refresh re-passing the full order validation), and the driver executes the path's jump-links itself — vaulting at the mandated 60-boost tier, banking dash spend to 70 when a jump lies ahead, and steering its own jump arcs toward the waypoint until landing.
- **Map collision data** for the online server is exported from the offline arena builder by hand — `__exportArenaCollision('<mapKey>')` in the browser console, pasted over that map's entry in `GENERATED_ARENA_COLLISION_DATA` (`shared/src/sim/arena.js`) — and must be re-run after any geometry change. Visual mesh is always rendered by the offline arena-build code on the client.
- **Pre-game loading.** Every unit visible in a pick (offline pickers) or in the lobby config (online queue room) starts its sprite-art downloads immediately — menu dead time absorbs the network wait — and GPU uploads are drip-fed one texture per frame. At match load, each Trio slot's 2nd/3rd roster units are additionally pre-built as complete (hidden) mechs, so a mid-match respawn is a pure swap-in: no construction, no decode, no upload during the fight.
- **Projectile broadphase (online sim).** Each map's obstacle boxes are indexed once into a 24-unit ground grid; every tick, each projectile (bullets, sniper rounds, laser bolts, every shotgun pellet) tests only the obstacles near its own flight segment for that tick instead of the whole map. The precise sweep test stays the final authority, so hit results are bit-identical to a full scan (differential-verified on all maps) — but dense maps (Factory: 400 boxes, 404 with the boundary walls) now cost the same as open ones, which removed the server-side lag during shotgun / high-RPM fights. Kei's hitscan beams and all non-weapon scans (bot sight, pathfinding, movement) deliberately keep the plain full scan.

- **The route grid as data (b1025c, user 2026-10-25).** A modelled map's nav grid is built once by `shared/tools/maps/navgrid_build.mjs` and served beside its model as `maps/<mapKey>_nav` (`NAV_FILE_EXT`: `.nav` on the release lines, `.wasm` on the PT site); the server's warm-up (`server/src/warmup.js`) and the client (`ensureOfflineNavGrid`, fetched at the map pick) read it instead of building — Compound's build was 3.4 s on a fast core and a minute on the free instance, a freeze no handshake survived (the "websocket error" at boot) and the offline match-start stall. The file carries a signature of the collision data it was built from (every box's and floor's fields), and a file whose signature is not the arena's is refused and the grid built; `shared/test/navdata.test.js` rebuilds every shipped file and compares it field for field, so a regenerated map or a changed builder fails the tests until the files are regenerated. Sizes: Compound 2.33 MB, Dust 0.75, Town 0.51, Spacecraft 0.32, Warehouse 0.09; a client downloads the one map it plays. For the file to be the client's grid too, the offline client's modelled arena now plays the shared obstacle list box for box — the server's perimeter slabs (`makeBoundaryObstacles`: 2 thick, 32 high, `topBuffer` 32) and the generated boxes with their floor tops marked — where it used to raise its own 28-high walls over the generated boxes (the two sims differed along the edges, a divergence noted in arena.js on 2026-10-09); the red edge line is drawn as before. `markFloorTops`, the arena's first-use pass that marks the boxes a floor lies on, asks the point index for the floors near each box instead of walking all of them: 811 → 133 ms on Compound, the same boxes marked (the count and the set checked on four maps).
- **Snapshots without unit tables (user 2026-10-25, the 5 GB month).** Every fighter in every snapshot carried its whole unit table (582 bytes, a quarter of a 2v2 snapshot); `buildSnapshotFor` now sends `unitKey` only and the client re-attaches the shared table on arrival (`connection.js`), as its prediction clone always did. 9,970 → 7,300 bytes per own-team 2v2 snapshot, 609 → 446 KB/s per client. The bot-intent fields and the rate are unchanged.
- **The lock moves to the living enemy (user 2026-10-25).** When the enemy a player is locked on dies and its Trio slot respawns, the lock moves to the other living enemy instead of following the slot to the fresh spawn. `applyInput`'s auto-retarget (2026-08-12) meant to do this on the death, but the server refills the slot in the very tick the unit dies, so by the player's next frame the slot held a living unit again and the lock went, silently, to the respawn behind its immunity; `respawnFighterNext` now moves every opponent's lock off the refilled slot when another living enemy exists (`shared/test/respawn_lock.test.js`). With none — Trio-1v1, a dead teammate — the lock keeps the slot as before. The offline client's `respawnSlotMech` already did this.
